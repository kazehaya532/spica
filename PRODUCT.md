# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

React, TypeScript, Vite, Three.js, and Astronomy Engine. The release remains a client-side application suitable for static deployment.

## Users

Curious beginners learning astronomy for personal interest or introductory education. The experience should remain approachable without limiting the depth available to motivated students.

## Product Purpose

Help beginners understand the night sky by connecting a location-aware 360-degree Star Map to concise lessons, object profiles, and knowledge checks. Success means a visitor can find a useful target tonight, recognize it, understand what it is, and continue into a relevant learning path.

## Positioning

Spica, “The Brightest Guide to the Universe,” links exploration and explanation directly: selectable objects in a calculated sky become entry points into beginner-focused lessons and quizzes rather than presenting a scientific simulation without a learning path.

## Operating Context

Visitors use the site on desktop or mobile to explore at their own pace. The Star Map begins in a global reference view and requests location only when the visitor chooses an observing feature. Approximate or manual coordinates remain in local browser storage.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Interactive 360-degree WebGL sky dome with drag, zoom, keyboard controls, search, object selection, fullscreen, time controls, and global or local observer modes.
- Magnitude-limited HYG stellar field renders a dense real-star field through visual magnitude 10.5 with position, brightness, and approximate color.
- Optional constellation lines and art, atmosphere, azimuthal and equatorial grids, deep-sky objects, and red night mode.
- Planets Tonight and Stargazing Tonight panels provide current visibility and local observing windows.
- Educational coverage includes astronomy basics, the solar system, stars and constellations, and deep-sky objects.
- Short quizzes provide immediate explanations and session-based progress.
- English is the initial language; content structure should remain localization-ready.
- No backend, accounts, cloud synchronization, telescope control, camera AR, weather, terrain modeling, or cloud-based location storage.
- Celestial data is curated for education and must not be represented as a complete scientific catalog.

## Brand Commitments

The product name is Spica and its promise is “The Brightest Guide to the Universe.” Scientific clarity, beginner-friendly language, and educational purpose must remain recognizable.

## Evidence on Hand

No supplied imagery, curriculum, testimonials, or proprietary astronomical data is available. The MVP may use clearly curated educational content but must not fabricate commercial or institutional claims.

## Product Principles

- Exploration should always lead to understanding.
- Scientific terms should be explained without talking down to the learner.
- The sky remains the primary interface, not decorative background.
- Every major visual interaction needs an accessible text-based path.
- Prefer a focused, reliable observing and learning guide over incomplete simulation breadth.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The application should support keyboard navigation, visible focus, reduced motion, readable contrast, touch interaction, and an accessible object list as an alternative to canvas-only exploration.
